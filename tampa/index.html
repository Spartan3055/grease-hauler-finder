What Tampa requires of food facilities

This is a short summary of what the city’s own pages say. It is not a complete statement of the ordinance. Read the official text and ask the Wastewater Department or your own advisor if you need to know how the rules apply to your kitchen.

  • The City of Tampa Grease Management Ordinance (Article VII of Chapter 26 of the City Code) was passed by City Council on November 16, 2006 and became effective on November 21, 2006. It “regulates the disposal of grease by food facilities and grease haulers.” Source: the city’s Grease Ordinance program page.
  • The purpose, in the city’s words, is “to establish uniform maintenance and monitoring requirements for controlling discharge of grease from food service facilities within the City limits that discharge into the City’s treatment works and for regulation of grease haulers operating within the city limits.” Source: the same program page.
  • The official Grease Ordinance FAQ states that each facility shall have its grease interceptor pumped when any of the following is true: the settled solids layer exceeds the invert of the outlet pipe (typically eight inches in depth); the total volume of captured grease and solid material displaces more than 25% of the interceptor’s capacity; or the interceptor is not retaining or capturing oils and greases.
  • For grease traps, the same FAQ says the facility is solely responsible for the cost of cleaning and maintenance. A facility may contract with a registered grease hauler or develop a written protocol and clean the trap itself. Cleaning must be done when captured grease and solids displace more than 25% of the trap’s total volume. The facility sets the frequency, “but all grease traps shall be opened, inspected, cleaned, and maintained at a minimum of once per week.”
  • The city’s forms page provides a Grease Management Log and says “it is very important to keep an accurate record of the pumping activity in your facility.” The grease hauler page says grease haulers are required to maintain pumping and disposal records on city-approved Grease Hauler manifest forms.
  • The FAQ says a grease interceptor or trap “will be inspected as necessary to assure compliance with this Ordinance.”

You can read Article VII in the city’s Code of Ordinances (the official ordinance text) via the city’s Article VII page, which links to Municode, Chapter 26, Article VII.

Enforcement and fines (official figures)

Quote these only as the city states them, and read the official remedies page for the full context, including appeal information. Source: Grease Management Ordinance Enforcement Remedies (page stamp: Updated 10/01/2023).

  • Administrative fines under Section 9-13(b)(1) of the City of Tampa Code, as stated on that page: a first violation “shall not exceed $1000.00 per day”; a repeat violation “shall not exceed $5000.00 per day”; and if the Code Enforcement Board determines that a violation is irreparable or irreversible in nature, the fine “shall not exceed $15,000.00 per violation.”
  • Criminal enforcement: the same page says the maximum penalty is “up to a $500.00 fine and or up to 60 days in jail for each offense,” and that “each day a violation occurs or continues shall be deemed a separate offense.”

The city’s program pages also carry their own notice that the website is informational, is not legal advice, and that information “may not be current.” Rely on the official pages and the ordinance, not on this summary.
