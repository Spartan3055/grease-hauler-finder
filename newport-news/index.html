What HRSD says about cleaning grease-control devices

This is a short summary of HRSD’s official FAQ and enforcement plan. Local city or county FOG programs may have additional rules. This is not legal advice and is not a complete statement of HRSD’s regulations.

  • On its Non-Residential Customer FAQs page, under “How do I clean my Grease Control Device (GCD)?”, HRSD states that GCDs of 50 gpm and smaller “may be approved to be cleaned by the Food Service Establishment with prior approval from your localities Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) Program.”
  • The same FAQ states: “Larger GCDs and outside devices must be cleaned by an HR FOG certified grease hauler who has an approved disposal location.”
  • HRSD links the permitted-hauler PDF as “a reference list of waste haulers who hold a Permit to discharge at HRSD receiving facilities.” The FAQ also says, in HRSD’s words: “Please note, this is not an exhaustive list of companies with the capability to clean and maintain grease control devices.” Confirm certification and disposal approval with HRSD or your local FOG program — do not treat this reference list as the only companies who can ever do the work, and do not skip the “certified hauler with an approved disposal location” requirement for larger and outdoor devices.
  • The FAQ also pointed to hrfog.com for cleaning frequencies and local FOG contacts. That site did not load reliably when we checked on November 18, 2026, so this page does not rely on it. Use the HRSD FAQ and your locality’s FOG program for current frequency rules.

Enforcement and fines (official figures)

Source: HRSD’s Pretreatment & Pollution Prevention Enforcement Response Plan, which states it is effective July 1, 1992 and revised July 1, 2025. The plan includes a Civil Penalty Schedule. It also states: “A civil penalty not to exceed $32,500 per violation may be assessed by HRSD if issued through a court in accordance with the Code of Virginia 1950, § 62.1-44.32 et seq.” The plan describes itself as guidance and says it does not abrogate HRSD’s enforcement discretion. Do not treat the $32,500 figure as an automatic fine.
