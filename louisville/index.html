What MSD requires of food-service establishments

This is a short summary of MSD’s official FOG page and FOG Management Policy. It is not a complete statement of the policy or the Wastewater/Stormwater Discharge Regulations. Read those documents, and ask MSD or your own advisor, if you need to know how the rules apply to your kitchen.

  • MSD’s official FOG page states that MSD’s FOG Management Policy “requires Food Service Establishments to use grease traps and/or grease interceptors to prevent FOG clogs.” Source: Fats, Oil, Grease (FOG) | MSD.
  • The same page says those devices “must be certified annually by an MSD approved Certified Grease Waste Hauler or Plumber.”
  • On the certified-hauler list itself, MSD writes: “Food Service Establishments must only utilize the services of an MSD approved Certified Grease Waste Hauler to pump out and/or remove grease waste solids from Grease Control Equipment.”
  • The FOG Management Policy (the policy document linked from the official FOG page; the PDF states it was adopted May 27, 2008) says FSEs shall install and maintain appropriately sized grease-control equipment at the FSE’s expense; waste removed from that equipment shall be disposed of at a properly permitted facility; FSEs shall not discharge FOG in amounts that cause a violation of MSD’s discharge regulations; and all FSEs in the MSD jurisdiction shall have an MSD-certified grease waste hauler or plumber complete a grease interceptor certification annually. Enforcement actions under the policy follow MSD’s Enforcement Response Plan.

Enforcement and fines (official figures)

Dollar amounts are published in MSD’s Wastewater/Stormwater Discharge Regulations. Source: MSD Wastewater/Stormwater Discharge Regulations (the official file hosted by MSD). Section 7.12, Administrative Fines, states that any user found to have violated any provision of the regulations, or permits or orders issued under them, may be fined “in an amount of not less than ($1000) per violation per day and for a maximum penalty not to exceed $32,500 per violation per day, or as subsequently approved by EPA.” It also states that each day of non-compliance “will be deemed a separate and distinct violation.” Section 7.13 provides that service may be terminated, after appropriate notice, where a violation is found to exist.

Those figures are general administrative-fine provisions in the regulations. Do not treat them as a ticket you will automatically receive, and do not treat this page as legal advice.
